how to be an octopus
in the here and now?

an embodied, experimental workshop with artist Miriam Simun.

001 / the workshop

what might we learn by thinking, sensing, and moving like an octopus? in this embodied, experimental workshop, artist Miriam Simun invites participants to explore the world from the perspective of the soft-bodied, many-armed cephalopod. through movement, sensory attention, imagination, and collective reflection, we explore what cephalopod intelligence might teach us about presence, distributed cognition, collective sensing, relationality, and uncertainty.

this here and now version moves between individual and group somatic exercises and group dialogue practice. "here and now" is a practice in which participants focus on what is happening between them in the present moment, using immediate feelings, perceptions, roles, and relational dynamics as the material for inquiry, to reveal the unconscious patterns the group is collectively producing.

expect sensory play, movement explorations, and moments of gentle discomfort as we probe the boundaries between self and environment, visibility and withdrawal, surface and depth.

how are we sensing collectively? how are we acting unconsciously? how can we use the body to shift ourselves and the group?

the session lasts four hours. we alternate between periods of silent movement, still dialogue, movement with dialogue, and reflection. we work alone, in pairs, and as a group. there will be an invitation to come into touch with yourself, your environment, and the others around you.

002 / the artist

Miriam Simun is a visual artist whose multidisciplinary practice uses science, somatics, scent, and humor to explore ways of knowing — the non-visual senses, interspecies relations, and radical reimaginings of life under ecological crisis. trained as a sociologist, she works as an "artist-as-fieldworker," conducting first-person research with communities from scientific laboratories to rewilded forests, freedivers to human pollinators — research that shapes the final artworks, spanning video, installation, painting, performance, and communal sensorial experiences.

their work has been presented at the New Museum, Gropius Bau, MIT List Center for Visual Art, Ars Electronica, Momenta Biennale, and the Bogotá Museum of Modern Art, among others. she is a recipient of grants and fellowships from Creative Capital, the Robert Rauschenberg Foundation, the Onassis Foundation, and the Gulbenkian Foundation, and has been featured in The New York Times, The New Yorker, BBC, and Forbes. Simun holds degrees from the MIT Media Lab, NYU's ITP, and the London School of Economics.
